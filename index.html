About

Who this channel is for, and why it exists.

I'm Stephen Hackett, and Biblical Studies and Reviews is where I work through Scripture the slow way — textual criticism, original languages, and the questions that come up when you actually sit with a passage instead of skimming it. Some of it is verse-by-verse study, some of it is reviewing tools and resources for people doing the same kind of digging.

This site is the home base for the channel: videos, a running blog for things that don't fit in a video, and a link to Digging Deep: Notetaker Deluxe, the Bible study notebook app that grew out of this work.

The Greek New Testament According to the Byzantine Text (2026)

Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine Textform · Guardian Press · Public domain

A full, 748-page edition of the Byzantine (Majority) Greek New Testament, aligned with the newly released Robinson–Pierpont 2026 text. It includes two comparative apparatuses: an edition apparatus tracing this text against eleven landmark Greek New Testaments (NA28, SBL, Tyndale House, Westcott–Hort, both Textus Receptus editions, and more), and a manuscript apparatus citing early papyri and uncial evidence for 50 significant variant units. Released into the public domain — free to copy, print, or redistribute.

Also available as a hyperlinked edition — real navigation built directly into the PDF, so you can jump straight to any book or chapter without scrolling: a bookmarks panel (book & chapter), a clickable Table of Contents, and a clickable Manuscript Apparatus Variant Unit Index linking all 50 highlighted variants to their evidence.

Want a printed copy?

There’s no official hardback edition yet (see the release video for why), but because this PDF is public domain, you can print your own copy on demand through a service like Snowfall Press — no minimum order, no setup fees, and no permission needed.

  1. Create a free Snowfall Press account, then go to Book Setup and click New Book.
  2. Select the new title and click Edit to open the Make-Ready page.
  3. Under the Body tab, click Load Body and upload the PDF above. Set the trim size to 6″ × 9″ — that's the size this edition was laid out at, and it's within Snowfall's printable range (4″×6″ up to 8.5″×11″).
  4. This release doesn’t include a separate cover file, so under the Cover tab, upload a simple front/spine/back cover of your own design — Snowfall wants both files as PDF X-1a.
  5. Order a single proof copy before printing in quantity. Snowfall specifically recommends this to catch bleed, font, or positioning issues before you commit to a full print run.
  6. Once the proof looks right, save the book and place your order. Snowfall charges only for printing, packaging, and shipping — paperback runs a few dollars a copy at their published pricing, before shipping.
