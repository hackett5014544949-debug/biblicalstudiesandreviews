LOOK CLOSER / TWO GREEK DANIELS

A translation can make
a reference explicit.

Brenton’s main Daniel text represents the version traditionally called Theodotion. It is not the earlier Old Greek. Compare these key references.

Do scholars agree about these rulers?

These early Hellenistic identifications are widely shared across conservative and critical scholarship. Agreement about the people is different from agreement about when Daniel was written or how prophecy works. A traditional view takes the visions as predictions from the sixth century BC; a common critical view places the book’s final form in the second century BC.

Neither view makes the Greek Torah’s production a clear subject of Daniel 11. Daniel gives us the political world; Aristeas gives us a translation story. A later date for Daniel would not, by itself, mean we should expect a reference to the Septuagint.

A SMALL WORD WITH A LONG JOURNEY

Passover across languages

The form of a borrowed word may preserve traces of translation before the written Greek Torah.

הַפֶּסַחHebrew · ha-pesaḥ→pasḥaAramaic→πάσχαGreek · pascha
What does this suggest?

The Greek form resembles Aramaic pasḥa more closely than Hebrew ha-pesaḥ. Lanier and Ross discuss this as evidence consistent with earlier oral translation traditions. One word cannot establish the full sequence, date, or setting of the written project.

01 → ∞

The Torah was the beginning.

The Greek Old Testament grew through multiple translations, original Greek compositions, and revisions. Other books were produced over centuries, with their exact dates, order, and places often disputed. Egypt is central, but Palestine also matters.

“The Septuagint” is a collection with a history—not a single translation event.

Why was the Torah
translated into Greek?

Saved only on this device
Reveal a model answer

The Greek Torah originated in the world of Hellenistic Judaism, probably in third-century-BC Egypt. Greek-speaking Jews had practical reasons to want their Scriptures in Greek; royal cultural or legal interests may also have helped. Aristeas celebrates the translation’s authority, but its details are not a secure transcript of the project. The precise commission, translators, and balance of motives remain uncertain.
