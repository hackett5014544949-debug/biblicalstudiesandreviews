    New Testament quotations of the Old

    Every quotation shown with the New Testament Greek, the Septuagint Greek, the Hebrew, and English for all three. Open a row to compare.

    Highlighted Greek words are shared by the New Testament and the LXX line. Verdicts are those of David Turpie, The Old Testament in the New (1868): Follows the LXX means the New Testament agrees with the LXX where the Hebrew differs. Follows the Hebrew is the reverse. Agrees with both means the two Old Testament texts and the New Testament line up. Free quotation means the LXX and Hebrew agree and the New Testament departs from both. Differs from both means all three texts differ. “Words in the LXX” is the share of the quoted New Testament words (leaving out the writer’s own words, such as “it is written”) that also appear in the LXX line, ignoring accents and word order. Words the LXX has that the New Testament leaves out do not lower it. It is a rough guide and does not replace the verdict.

      About this tool

      • Every entry comes from the guide Where the Dead Sea Scrolls and the Septuagint Align Against the Masoretic Text by Stephen Hackett. The guide says each variant has support from at least one Dead Sea Scroll, even where it does not name the scroll.
      • English for the Masoretic Text is Young's Literal Translation or the King James Version, and each entry says which. Where either one already follows the variant, the entry says so and gives a literal rendering instead. The Septuagint is Brenton's translation unless a line names another source.
      • Hebrew is shown as consonants only and was checked against the Leningrad Codex. The guide notes that its vowel points may not always be flawless.
      • Notes summarize the guide's notes, and an “Open point” marks a spot where a source could not be reconciled. Sources are the BHS apparatus, the Lexham Textual Notes, the NET Bible notes, NETS, and the commentaries named beside each note.
      • The NT quotations tab uses public-domain sources: the Robinson-Pierpont Byzantine text for the New Testament, Swete's edition for the Greek Septuagint, Brenton's English Septuagint, the Leningrad Codex for the Hebrew, Young's Literal Translation for the Hebrew in English, and the King James Version for the New Testament in English. The list of quotations and the verdict on each come from Turpie (1868). A few short notes are added.
      • New Testament quotations on the scroll tab are from the King James Version.
